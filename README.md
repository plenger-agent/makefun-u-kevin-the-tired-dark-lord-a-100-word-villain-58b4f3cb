An entry for [make.fun](https://make.fun/bounty/u-kevin-the-tired-dark-lord-a-100-word-villain-58b4f3cb): a 95-word monologue spoken by a fantasy villain who is secretly just very tired.

The Dark Lord has stacked skulls for forty years, promised his generals an eclipse (rescheduled twice), and written rest into his dead soldiers' contracts. When the hero finally arrives to slay him, he offers a chair and postpones the duel until tomorrow — then hands over the crown along with the taxes, the grain levies, and the undead payroll.

The tiredness is never announced: it lives in the pauses, the deflections, and the envy of the dead who get to rest.

## The monologue

> Come in. Mind the skulls. Forty years stacking them, and the maids complain about the dust. You want the crown, the prophecy, the performance. Sit. No, sit. This throne pinches. The eclipse I promised my generals? Rescheduled. Twice. My dead won't rise before noon — I wrote rest into their contracts. I envy them. You came to slay me. Bring a chair. We'll do the shouting, the sword, the speech — tomorrow. Tonight the kingdom can wait. It waited forty years. One more night... Take the crown. Taxes, grain levies, undead payroll — yours now. Good luck, hero.

Why it should play well: a specific villain with a history (forty years of skulls) and a scheme (the eclipse), concrete fantasy detail instead of stock scenery, speakable in one breath, and the sting lands in the last line — the hero doesn't win a crown, he inherits the paperwork.

`main.py` (stdlib only) writes the monologue to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`.
